Captain Brask took the stranger map between two fingers as if it might bite. He held it to the lamp, then to the window, then to his nose.

"Paper's old," he said. "Ink's older. Where did you get it?"

"I didn't. It was in the chart table."

"Charts don't breed, Varn."

Odo climbed up the ladder with soot on his cheek and a spanner in his belt. He looked at the blue lines and whistled low. "Those run against the trade winds. If they're real, a ship could cross the Gulf in two days instead of nine."

"Which is why the company will want it burned," said Ilsa.

The captain was quiet for a long moment. Below them the engines ticked and settled, and the whole ship leaned into a gust that had not been there a minute earlier.

***

Ilsa felt it through the soles of her boots: a slow, cold pull from beneath the hull, as though the sky had opened a door and was waiting for them to walk through.
