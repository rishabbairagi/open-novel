He arrived at last, limping, dusty, and astonishingly pale, with a cut on his chin and hair that had gone grey at the edges. He drank a glass of wine, ate a little, and asked them to let him tell it from the beginning.

The full-sized machine had been waiting in his laboratory. When he climbed into the saddle and pushed the lever, the world outside the windows began to flicker. Day and night alternated faster and faster until they blurred into a grey twilight. The sun became a streak of fire, the moon a pale band, and the walls of his house melted away like breath on glass.

"I felt a sickening dread of falling headlong," he told them. "I pushed the lever over, and the night came like the turning out of a lamp."

***

When he finally eased the lever back, he found himself in a garden under a vast, strange sky. Strange white statues stood among the rhododendrons, and a crowd of small, delicate people ran toward him, laughing in a language he could not yet follow.
